# Current empirical abstract — working draft

2026-09-30. This is a candidate for a joint paper, not a submission-ready paper or a claim of completed independent human validation. The [historical protocol abstract](abstract.md) is retained unchanged.

Role prompting and access to external evidence are distinct interventions in LLM multi-agent systems. We conducted two comparisons using Qwen3:14b and an Isolated Agent Runtime that enforces input boundaries and typed artifact communication. Study 1 evaluated five conditions on 30 fixed, public-length- and structure-selected HotpotQA distractor development questions, completing 150 unique cells and 510 successful native generations with one repetition. On the pre-specified 24 questions not observed in the preceding pilot, the mean Answer F1 difference between C3 (neutral roles, partitioned documents) and C2 (diverse roles, full access) was −0.1564. Because this contrast changes both role and access, it does not isolate the causal effect of information partitioning. Citation statistics and preliminary saved-output case inspection motivated a hypothesis concerning worker extraction, publication, and synthesis. Study 2 fixed the existing C3 worker outputs and compared output-only input, added cited source excerpts, and added token-length-matched neutral text. All 9 smoke and 72 main native responses completed. On the 24 previously observed main questions, Answer F1 was 0.5666, 0.5805, and 0.5805, respectively. The excerpt condition improved by +0.0139 over output-only input but had a mean difference of zero against the neutral-text control. Thus, these observations did not support an excerpt-specific mean answer improvement in this sample; they neither establish the mechanism nor refute information loss generally. Limitations include a small selected sample, one model and repetition, unequal total computation, an exploratory follow-up on previously observed questions, and pending independent human review. We make no population-wide HotpotQA or general LLM claim.
